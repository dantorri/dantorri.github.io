The question I worked on

Our robot was designed for mixed land-water environments. I worked on RF and Wi-Fi integration and investigated low-frequency RF as an approach to telemetry through water.

This made communications part of the mechanical and electrical design problem. The antenna has to live somewhere on the robot, alongside motor electronics, power wiring, and a body intended to operate in water.

I focused on frequency choice, antenna placement, and EMI mitigation. I treated the radio, antenna, power electronics, and vehicle packaging as a single system.

Three parts of the investigation

Low-frequency RF

I investigated low-frequency radio for the underwater telemetry problem. The goal was to understand what could serve the robot’s communication needs across its changing operating environment.

Antenna placement

The antenna location is constrained by the body, packaging, and changing waterline. I considered placement alongside the body layout and operating environment.

Electrical interference

Motor controllers and power distribution share the vehicle with the communications electronics. EMI mitigation was part of my work on making those subsystems coexist.

From radio to robot

This work sits alongside my embedded control work on the same platform. Above-water Wi-Fi command execution and the submerged RF investigation address different parts of the robot’s operating environment.

That is what interests me about amphibious robotics: a communications question becomes a packaging question, an electrical question, and a controls question. Following those connections is a large part of the engineering.

Research scope. RF and Wi-Fi integration, with investigation of low-frequency radio, antenna placement, and EMI. Quantitative underwater link results are not included in this account.
