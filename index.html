Jumping exoskeleton test frameAssistive robotics

At Virginia Tech’s Assistive Robotics Lab, I designed and prototyped a constrained linear frame for a high-power jumping exoskeleton and assisted with underactuated mechanisms. The work brought mechanical test fixtures, motor control, and elastic energy storage into the same system.

5-DoF robotic armKinematics & vision

I implemented forward and inverse kinematics for a five-degree-of-freedom arm with a gripper, then explored OpenCV feedback for target alignment. The vision control was an attempted extension, rather than a validated closed-loop result.

November 2025–February 2026

A little background.

I came to robotics through mechanical engineering. The software kept getting more interesting.

I graduated from Virginia Tech in May 2026 with a B.S. in Mechanical Engineering, a major in Robotics and Mechatronics, and a minor in Computer Science. I’m interested in the whole system: mechanisms, electronics, control, and the code that makes it move.

Outside individual projects, I’ve led mechanical design and mentored younger builders in Virginia Tech’s Competitive Robotics Organization. Our 2024 SoutheastCon team took first in hardware and design.
